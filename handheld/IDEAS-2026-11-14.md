# A ternary fantasy handheld (filed 2026-11-14, not scheduled)

Kham, 2026-11-14 20:25, after asking whether Trytris GBA is the first ternary GBA game: "a proper ternary gba or psp just... isn't a thing, and would be *wild*."

**The idea:** a ternary *platform*, not just ternary apps on binary hardware. The studio already has both halves:
- **Triton / TritOS:** a working balanced-ternary machine (assembler, OS, apps).
- **1337Boy:** proof the studio can make "a handheld that is its own console" in one file.
- **Trytris:** the obvious launch title. It already runs on Triton (App 11), and its board is a balanced-ternary state.

**Sketch (Kehai, unrefined):**

- Display where each pixel is a trit: ▲ / · / ▽ glyph-pixels, or three brightness levels per channel. Even the picture is balanced ternary.

**The honest asterisk** (Kham agreed it's the same as all our ternary work): it runs on binary silicon. It's a real ternary platform, not real ternary hardware.

**Next step, whenever:** decide whether it's a Triton profile (Triton + a display/sound/input spec) or its own machine. Then an argon-spec.

**Kham's addition (22:27):** "Pixels that are trits, rgb as -0, 1, +1, little triangle physical lcd subpixels"
- Each color channel is one trit: −1 off, 0 half, +1 full. 3³ = **27 colors per pixel**, so one pixel = 3 trits and a 9-trit tryte = exactly 3 pixels.
- Triangle subpixels that alternate up/down: the screen itself is a Trytris grid (▲▽▲▽), with the same parity rule as the game board.

**Toward real hardware (22:34, Kham: "given time and resources, really do it"):** a ladder, each rung useful by itself.
1. Browser platform (Triton profile + trit display/sound): studio pace, days.
2. FPGA: Triton core with binary-encoded trits, driving a real screen. Hobby-feasible; binary silicon underneath.
3. Real trit display: a hand-built panel of triangle-arranged RGB LEDs, each channel driven at 3 levels. Hobby-feasible. A custom triangular LCD needs a fab, so it isn't.
4. Real ternary logic: shown at tiny scale (Triador: 3-trit CPU from DG403 analog switches). A whole handheld that way would be a long haul; a ternary display controller or ALU is plausible.

**Custom silicon is real at hobby prices (checked 2026-11-14):**
- **Tiny Tapeout:** about €50 a tile (~1,000 logic cells); a single-tile design plus a copy of the chip has been €185 including shipping. **Analog tiles exist** ($40/pin for the first four analog pins). That's the door to *real* multi-level ternary signals on silicon, not just binary-encoded trits.
- **wafer.space:** GF180, 1,000 ICs for $7,000.
- A first step could be one real ternary gate or a tiny trit ALU on an analog Tiny Tapeout tile.

**Forebears (checked 2026-11-14):** the Setun line was a university project, not a state secret, and it's documented in English:
- Brusentsov & Ramil Alvarez, "Ternary Computers: The Setun and the Setun 70" (SoRuCom 2006, IFIP): https://dl.ifip.org/db/conf/ifip9/sorucom2006/BrusentsovA06.pdf
- Russian Virtual Computer Museum: https://www.computer-museum.ru/english/setun.htm and the Brusentsov Hall of Fame page.
- Hardware: ferrite cores + semiconductor diodes. **Two cores per trit**, so Setun also held a trit in a pair of two-state elements: the same asterisk as ours. Setun-70: 6-trit trytes, two-stack architecture, later DSSP.
- Brusentsov first built and proved a ternary adder, then the whole machine. Same order as our "one gate on an analog tile first".

**Rung 0 as an Electronic Japery episode (Kham, 23:26):**
- **Magnet wire:** miles of it, from disused transformer windings.
- **Toroids:** salvage. ATX power supplies, CFL/LED driver boards, CRT sets, snap-on cable ferrites, motherboard chokes. Watch the material: painted iron-powder cores (yellow/white, green/blue) saturate softly, while bare grey/black ferrite saturates sharply, and that's what a trit cell wants. Test empirically.
- **Pulses:** TENS unit (already in his notes as a "measured pulse driver") or a 555. Or the quartz clock chips from the hoard (the Bastard's clock-cascade idea) with a divider. Mind inductive kickback into the TENS, and never be wired to yourself while it's driving coils.
- **Seeing it without a scope:** a bi-color LED per trit (+ red, − green, 0 dark) shows the state directly. For pulse shapes, his "homemade oscilloscope" project idea finally gets a job (sound-card scope if the clock is slow enough). Fallback: a cheap DSO kit.
- **Japery option:** derive the cell from first principles first, then compare with Brusentsov's. Fits the "no guides" rule; Setun becomes the answer key, not the instructions.

**Kham (23:33): "3 trits, the first one on its own to learn the basics and then the other two at the same time."**
- 3 trits = 27 states, −13..+13. (Not a tryte: Triton's tryte is 9 trits, Setun-70's was 6.)
- **3 trits is exactly one trit-pixel:** each trit drives one channel of an RGB LED at −1 off / 0 half / +1 full, giving 27 colors. The hand-wound memory becomes the first pixel of the ternary display.

**Kham (23:35): 1 trit alone, then a jig, then 8 at once → 9 trits = one Triton tryte.** "When I have a jig that supports making multiple trits, that's one step away from scaling."
- What the jig has to give: matched cells (same core material and size, same turn counts, same winding tension and placement). In analog, cell-to-cell variation is the enemy.
- Suggestion: test the jig on 2–3 cells and check they match before committing to the batch of 8.
- Precedent: Setun's cores were hand-wound, then mounted onto blocks by technicians, and the design went into production at the Kazan plant (~50 machines). The jig is how a one-off becomes a process.

**REAL NUMBERS (23:50, after reading the paper Kham fetched + Russian sources):**
- The Brusentsov & Alvarez 2006 paper is architecture/history only, with no circuit. Useful facts: ~2,000 magnetic elements per Setun at 3 rubles 50 kopeks each; 3 defective elements replaced in the first year; the prototype ran 17 years. Setun: 9-trit commands, 18-trit registers, 162 nine-trit words of operating memory + drum. Setun 70: 6-trit trytes, 81 operations. The paper also gives the balanced-ternary add and multiply tables.
- **Winding table** (Russian Virtual Computer Museum, "Из истории создания троичных цифровых машин в МГУ", via search snippet): PEV-2 enamelled wire 0.1 mm; **input windings 12 turns each; output 52 turns (64 for "powerful" cells); power winding 0.5 mm wire, 5 turns (6)**. The physical parameters (ferrite grade, core size, turns, pulse frequency/amplitude/shape, diode bias) were fixed "forever" and never changed in production.
- Clock: **200 kHz**. One trit = **two binary ferrite-diode cells** (2-bit code, 4th state unused), built on Gutenmakher's binary ferrite-diode cell (a transformer-type magnetic-amplifier relay).
- Cores must be **rectangular-hysteresis-loop (ППГ) ferrite**. Ordinary salvaged power toroids are NOT square-loop and may not hold a state. Sourcing: vintage core-memory boards, Soviet ППГ ferrite rings.
- Hobbyist precedent: nedoPC forum thread "МЭВМ «Сетунь» цифровые элементы на ферритах" (viewtopic.php?t=18829) reproduces Setun elements; output pulses detected with an LED; D9D diode, 50 Ω load. Couldn't fetch the full thread from here.
- Revised ballpark: ~12×inputs + 52 + 5 ≈ 70–80 turns per cell → **~150 turns per trit**, mostly 0.1 mm wire. Core size still unknown.
- TENS is far too slow for 200 kHz; a slower clock weakens the output pulses (EMF ∝ dΦ/dt). Plan on a 555/oscillator in the tens-to-200 kHz range. The LED shows the pulses; the multimeter can't.

**Kham (00:01, Nov 15): "Why not just... make Triton, actually make it? Not a Setun clone; readily available 3-state components repurposed as load-bearing hardware."** On to something, with these refinements:
- **The advantage:** Triton has a spec, two interpreters, tests and an OS *before* any hardware. The JS interpreter is the golden reference, and every piece of hardware gets verified by differential (same method as the GBA ports). Setun had to invent its software after the machine.
- **Real ternary from available parts:**
  - analog-switch ternary multiplexer as the universal element (Triador: DG403 only);
  - ±V balanced rails + window comparators / complementary transistors (+V / 0 / −V);
  - center-stable polarized relays (a relay Triton: slow, visible, very Japery);
  - phase-encoded logic: parametron cousin. Goto's parametron stored bits as the phase of a subharmonic oscillation; a 1/3-subharmonic oscillator has three stable phases. This is the "3-phase is a cousin" intuition. Unknown to me whether anyone built one.
  - Caveat: tri-state buffers (0/1/Hi-Z) aren't ternary values by themselves, but with a midpoint pull network plus window comparators they can be abused into trits.
- **Scale honesty:** TritOS Desktop needs Triton 3: nine 27-trit registers and 3^14 = 4,782,969 trytes of RAM by default. Memory has to be binary chips holding 2 bits per trit (Setun did the same). Triton 2.1 (9-trit addresses, 19,683 trytes) is the Setun-scale target for an all-ternary-logic CPU (Setun: ~2,000 elements).
- **Ship of Theseus path:** FPGA Triton 3 runs TritOS first → swap in real ternary pieces one at a time (ALU slice → register file → display), each swap passing the differential tests → eventually the logic is real ternary and the OS never stopped running.

**Kham (00:07): a 3-position toggle is a free trit.** Yes, as a manually set one:
- ON-OFF-ON (SPDT center-off) toggle: +V on one throw, −V on the other, common = output, plus a resistor from output to ground so center reads as true 0 (otherwise it floats).
- It's stable memory, but only a hand can write it. Perfect as a **front panel** (Altair-style trit switches for entering programs) and as a test fixture for every other rung.
- The self-writing version of the same switch is the center-stable polarized relay: the toggle with a coil.
- Square-loop explainer is in the chat; short form: a square-loop core snaps between two saturated states and *stays* there (remanence ≈ saturation). Power ferrite drifts back toward zero when the drive stops. Square loop = 2 stable states, so a Setun trit = 2 cores.

**Kham (00:13): "A mechanical, solenoid-switch-based ternary computer."** Sound principle: electromechanical logic is how computing started (Zuse's Z3, 1941, was a relay machine with about 2,600 relays).
- **Natively ternary, no asterisk:** a 3-position solenoid switch / center-stable polarized relay holds one full trit in ONE part. No 2-binary-cells-per-trit encoding (unlike Setun's cores or bits-in-SRAM).
- The design question is **stability:** spring-centered (0 is free, ±1 needs holding current) vs latching/detented (all three hold with no power = real memory). Registers want latching; logic can be spring-centered.
- **Speed:** relays switch in milliseconds, so the clock is roughly 5–100 Hz. Not TritOS; a Triton 2.1 subset or simple programs, front panel + trit LEDs. Loud and visible = great video.
- **Rough scale (estimate, not measured):** a 1-trit balanced adder ≈ 6–10 three-position relays; 9-trit adder ≈ 60–90; one latching relay per register trit. A minimal Triton-2.1-class CPU lands near 1,000–3,000 relays (Setun ≈ 2,000 elements, Z3 ≈ 2,600 relays).
- **Japery tie:** the solenoid that was meant to be the first project comes back as the computer's actual element, and the PeBFOP coil-winding knowledge applies directly.

**Kham (00:16): "3-post transistors are a thing, aren't they??"**
- Three legs ≠ three states: an ordinary transistor's legs are input (base/gate), output and common. Its job is a valve.
- **But two transistors make a trit:** one NPN + one PNP (or n- and p-MOSFET) in series between +V and −V give +V / 0 / −V at the middle. That's the textbook balanced-ternary inverter (STI); the 0 needs a middle current path or bias. 2 cheap parts per trit gate, on a breadboard, from the hoard.
- **Real ternary transistors exist in research:** UNIST/Samsung "T-CMOS" (band-to-band tunneling gives a third stable state on ordinary CMOS wafers, verified on 300 mm wafers; 6-transistor ternary SRAM cells, 2025). Also feedback FETs and biristor threshold switches that hold three states. Not a buyable hobby part.

**Kham (00:33): NPN/PNP as the main line: faster, cheaper, easier than relays, and no less pure.** Agreed:
- **Purity:** the signal on the wire *is* three-valued (+V/0/−V). No binary encoding anywhere, same as the relays. (Transistors are continuous inside, but so are binary ones; purity is about the representation.)
- **Speed:** discrete-transistor logic can plausibly run kHz to low MHz; Setun ran 200 kHz. Relays: ~5–100 Hz.
- **Engineering challenges to plan for:**
  1. **Holding a trit** needs a ternary latch (two cross-coupled inverters), and the middle 0 is the touchy state, held by bias, not by a hard rail.
  2. **Noise margins:** three levels share one voltage swing, so resistor tolerances matter.
  3. **Static power:** the 0 state can draw current through bias resistors (part of why T-CMOS research exists).
- **Scale (rough guess):** a Triton-2.1-class CPU is likely thousands to tens of thousands of transistors → PCBs, not breadboards. First milestones: 1 STI → a ternary latch that holds 0 → 1-trit adder.
- Relay Triton stays the showpiece; NPN/PNP becomes the workhorse.

**Kham (00:37): "PCBs with transistors and support components? Those are *nothing*."** Right. Correction to my framing: PCBs aren't a hurdle.
- Prototype boards from the usual hobby fabs cost a few dollars for a small batch, and their assembly services place SMD transistors/resistors in bulk. Tens of thousands of transistors = design once, order assembled.
- **Trit-slice architecture** (like 1970s bit-slice CPUs): design ONE board that does one trit of ALU + register, order 9 or 27 identical copies, plug them into a backplane. One design to debug, mass-produced by the fab.
- Free tooling: KiCad. Each slice is verified against the Triton JS reference like everything else.

**Kham (00:49): "The stable 0 thing... what about a tiny capacitor?"** Half yes, with a neat inversion:
- **A capacitor *in a latch*** smooths noise and slows drift (a flywheel), but if the circuit's middle point is a hilltop, it only slows the fall. The real fix is making 0 a **valley**: active restoration toward 0 with hysteresis around both thresholds, i.e. a **ternary Schmitt trigger** (window comparator with feedback).
- **A capacitor *as* the memory** = ternary DRAM: store +V / 0 / −V as charge, read with a window comparator, refresh periodically. **Inversion:** leakage drains ±V toward ground, so on a capacitor **0 is the MOST stable state** and ±1 are the ones that need refresh. Balanced ternary's zero is the natural rest state of charge storage.
- Split: static latches (Schmitt-style) for registers, capacitor ternary DRAM for bulk memory.
